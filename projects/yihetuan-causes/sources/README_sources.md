# Sources — provenance and reliability

Question: 义和团运动为什么在 1898–1900 年在华北（山东、直隶）迅速兴起？
Why did the Yihetuan (Boxer) movement arise and spread so rapidly in North China, 1898–1900?

## Source 1 (secondary — no OCR needed)

**Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Boxer Rebellion"** — authoritative reference article.

Quote used (key causes):
> "In the late 19th century, because of growing economic impoverishment, a series of unfortunate
> natural calamities, and unbridled foreign aggression in the area, the Boxers began to increase
> their strength in the provinces of North China."
> "Christian missionary activities helped provoke the Boxers; Christian converts flouted
> traditional Chinese ceremonies and family relations; and missionaries pressured local officials
> to side with Christian converts … in local lawsuits and property disputes."

URL: https://www.britannica.com/event/Boxer-Rebellion

Why reliable: Britannica is a long-established reference encyclopedia; the article reflects the
synthesis of modern scholarship on the subject. It is a **tertiary** source, however, so per the
assignment it is used as framing but is *not* treated as sufficient evidence on its own — the
primary/contemporary source below carries the specific claim.

## Source 2 (needs OCR — the OCR'd source)

**Paul H. Clements, *The Boxer Rebellion: A Political and Diplomatic Review* (New York: Columbia
University, 1915), ch. "Causes of the Rebellion," printed p. 39.** Studies in History, Economics
and Public Law; the author was a Fellow in International Law at Columbia.

Digitized (public domain) by the Library of Congress and Internet Archive:
- LOC item: https://www.loc.gov/item/16014237
- IA scan: `boxerrebellionpo66clemuoft` → page scan used: `sources/raw/ia_42.jpg` (leaf n42 = printed p.39), **unchanged**.

OCR'd text: `sources/processed/page-39_vision-ocr.md` (vision method) compared with IA retro-OCR in `ocr/ocr_results_comparison.md`.

Quote used (key causes):
> "The Powers, in their mad scramble for land, did not take into account the rights of the
> Chinese … In 1899, however, the united opinion of Europe was that China would soon disappear as
> a sovereign entity, dismembered and divided … the European demands had been comparatively easy
> of realization. Besides acquisitions of territory, there were numberless other demands …
> consisting mainly of religious and commercial concessions, railway grants, and heavy indemnities
> for every conceivable item … Europe seemed under the impression that China was an immense
> golden harvest to be reaped by the first comer."

Why reliable: Published by Columbia University Press as part of a scholarly series, written by a
trained international-law scholar fourteen years after the rebellion, and built on the published
US diplomatic record (he cites *U.S. Foreign Relations* and **China No. 1 / China No. 3** for
1899/1900). It is a credible, citable contemporary academic source. **Caveat:** it is an
American, 1915, "scramble-for-concessions" interpretation of causation; it should be read
alongside the ecological and missionary accounts in Source 1 and the placard text below.

## Supporting primary-adjacent text (flagged: verified second-hand only — NOT counted as one of the two required sources)

A Boxer placard reported in the local history 《天津政俗沿革记》 and quoted in modern reference
material:
> "天无雨、地焦旱、全是教堂止住天" ("No rain from heaven, the earth is parched and scorched —
> it is entirely the churches that block up the sky.")

This connects drought → anti-Christian blame, which is the mechanism linking Source 1's
"natural calamities" to Source 2's "religious concessions." I have **not** yet verified this
placard text against an original image/scan; it is quoted second-hand, so I keep it as
support and flag it in `questions.md` as a follow-up OCR target.
